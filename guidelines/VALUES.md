# Values Framework

Use this guide to select meaningful lessons without forcing morals onto the source material. Each section should usually focus on one main value.

## Core values

- **Tawakkul — trust in Allah:** do what is right and make a sincere effort while trusting Allah.
- **Sabr — patience:** keep doing what is right when something is difficult; explain the word in simple English.
- **Shukr — gratitude:** notice blessings and say thank you to Allah and to people.
- **Sidq — truthfulness:** tell the truth, even when it is hard.
- **Amanah — trustworthiness:** care for what someone has trusted you with.
- **Rahmah — mercy and kindness:** treat people and animals gently.
- **Adl — fairness and justice:** do not treat people unfairly; listen and act honestly.
- **Ihsan — doing good well:** try to do what is right with care.
- **Tawbah — returning to Allah after a mistake:** admit the mistake, seek forgiveness, and try to make things right, when appropriate to the story.
- **Shaja'ah — courage:** do the right thing even when you feel afraid.
- **Tawadu' — humility:** do not think you are better than others.
- **Generosity:** share and help without showing off.
- **Respect and family care:** speak kindly and help at home, while keeping advice age-appropriate and never implying that a child must accept unsafe behaviour.
- **Seeking knowledge:** ask questions, learn, and listen carefully.
- **Keeping promises:** do what you said you would do, when it is right and possible.

## How to choose a value

1. Identify the action or event clearly supported by the source.
2. Ask what value is genuinely demonstrated by that event.
3. Select one main value; secondary values may be noted for parents.
4. Phrase the lesson as a positive, simple sentence a child can remember.
5. Avoid overclaiming cause and effect. Do not imply that patience means never asking for help, or that trust in Allah means avoiding sensible action.

## Age-appropriate practice

Examples: share a toy, tell the truth about a small mistake, thank someone, wait for a turn, help tidy up, speak gently, or care for an animal safely with an adult. Do not assign activities involving danger, strangers, money, food restrictions, or religious obligations beyond a young child's understanding.
