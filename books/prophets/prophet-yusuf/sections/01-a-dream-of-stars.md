# Section 1 — A Dream of Stars

**Status:** Reviewed — parent-approved on 2026-10-20. The story text below remains unchanged.

Have you ever had a dream that you wanted to tell someone about? Perhaps you woke up remembering something surprising. When something is important to us, we often want to share it with someone we trust.

The Qur'an begins this part of the story with Prophet Yusuf (peace be upon him) speaking to his father, Prophet Ya'qub (peace be upon him).

Yusuf told his father about a dream. In it, he had seen **eleven stars, the sun, and the moon**. He had seen them bowing low before him.

Eleven stars! The sun! The moon!

It was an unusual dream, and Yusuf shared it with his father. The Qur'an does not tell us what colour the stars were or where Yusuf was when he saw the dream. We do not need to imagine extra details to understand the important part: Yusuf told his father what he had seen.

Ya'qub listened. Then he gave his son careful advice. He told Yusuf not to tell his brothers about the dream, because they might make a plan against him. Ya'qub knew that this was something Yusuf should be careful about sharing with them.

His father also reminded him that Shaytan (Satan) is an enemy to people. The warning helped Yusuf understand that not everyone will always choose what is good. Sometimes people may let jealousy lead them towards unkind choices. Ya'qub's advice was meant to protect his son from harm.

But Ya'qub's words were not only a warning. He also gave Yusuf hopeful news. He told him that Allah would choose him, teach him to understand the meaning of events, and complete His favour upon him and the family of Ya'qub. He reminded Yusuf of the favour Allah had given to their forefathers, Ibrahim and Ishaq (peace be upon them).

Yusuf's dream was only the beginning of a much longer story. At this point, he did not yet know all the events that would come. The Qur'an does not tell us every detail of what father and son did after their conversation, so we will not add actions or words that are not in the account.

Still, we can learn from this moment. Yusuf shared something important with his father, and his father answered with both caution and hope. He warned Yusuf about a real concern, but he also reminded him of Allah's care and wisdom.

When you have a question or something important to share, it is good to find a trusted grown-up who will listen and help you think about what to do. Good advice is not always just saying, “Everything will be easy.” Sometimes it means helping us be careful. Sometimes it means reminding us to keep hope and do what is right.

And so the story begins: with a dream of eleven stars, the sun, and the moon—and a father's thoughtful advice.

**For curious listeners (ages 5–6):** The Qur'an returns to Yusuf's dream near the end of the story, in Surah Yusuf 12:100. The Qur'an does not give the stars individual names, so we will not add names to them.

**The lesson:** We can listen carefully to wise advice from a trusted grown-up.

**Talk together:** If you had something important to share, which trusted grown-up could you talk to?

**Try it today:** Share a question or something you have been thinking about with a trusted grown-up, and listen to their advice.
