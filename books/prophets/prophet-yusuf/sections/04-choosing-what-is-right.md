# Section 4 — Choosing What Is Right

As Yusuf grew older, Allah gave him wisdom and knowledge. Yusuf was living in the home of the man from Egypt who had bought him. Then he faced a serious test.

The Qur'an tells us that a woman in the household tried to lead Yusuf into doing something wrong. She closed the doors and called him towards her. Yusuf refused. He sought refuge in Allah and remembered the kindness of the man who had given him a place to live.

Yusuf did not agree to do what was wrong. He turned away and moved towards the door. As they reached it, his shirt was torn from behind. The woman's husband was there at the door.

The woman accused Yusuf of being the one who had done wrong. Yusuf said that she had been the one who tried to lead him into wrongdoing. A witness from her family explained that the tear in Yusuf's shirt would help show what had happened. If the shirt was torn at the front, that would point one way; if it was torn at the back, it would point another.

When the shirt was seen to be torn from the back, this was evidence that Yusuf had been moving away. The account showed that Yusuf had spoken truthfully. The man in the household recognised that the accusation was not as the woman had claimed.

The matter did not end there. People in the city began talking about what had happened. The woman arranged a gathering with other women. The Qur'an mentions that they were given knives for cutting food and were astonished when they saw Yusuf. The story then returns to the pressure placed on Yusuf to do wrong.

Yusuf asked Allah for help. He said that prison would be better for him than doing what they were urging him to do. He wanted Allah to protect him from wrongdoing. The Qur'an tells us that he was imprisoned for a time, even though the evidence had shown that he had not done the wrong he was accused of.

This is a difficult part of Yusuf's story, so we can tell it simply: Yusuf was pressured to do something wrong, but he refused and asked Allah for help. He was treated unfairly, yet he did not choose to do wrong just to make the difficult situation end.

Children should remember that if anyone pressures them to do something that feels wrong or unsafe, they can say no and tell a trusted grown-up. They do not have to manage a frightening or confusing situation alone. Asking for help is a wise thing to do.

Yusuf's choice did not make every problem disappear. He still faced an unfair outcome. But he chose what was right, and the story continues beyond the prison.

**A word to know:** *Integrity* means choosing to do what is right, even when it is difficult.

**For curious listeners (ages 5–6):** Surah Yusuf 12:26–28 describes how the tear in Yusuf's shirt was used as evidence. The story's lesson is not that every difficult situation can be solved by one clue, but that truth and evidence matter.

**The lesson:** We should seek Allah's help and choose what is right.

**Talk together:** If someone pressures you to do something wrong, which trusted grown-up could help you?

**Try it today:** Practise saying, “No, I don't want to do that,” and remember that you can ask a trusted grown-up for help.
