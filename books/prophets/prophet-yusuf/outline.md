# Outline — Prophet Yusuf: A Story of Patience and Forgiveness

**Book ID:** `prophet-yusuf`  
**Status:** **Skeleton — needs parent review**  
**Audience:** Ages 3–6; caregiver read-aloud  
**Length plan:** 10 sections, each approximately 4–5 minutes  
**Primary narrative:** Qur'an, Surah Yusuf 12:4–101  
**Source status:** Primary Qur'anic passage and selected tafsir context checked on 2026-10-20. See [sources.md](sources.md).

## Book promise

A gentle, source-led account of how Prophet Yusuf faces painful and difficult events, remains committed to what is right, uses the knowledge Allah gives him to help others, and eventually reunites with his family. The story must not imply that patience prevents hardship or guarantees an easy life.

## Age fit and read-aloud approach

- Keep the main story concrete, calm, and clear for ages 3–4.
- Use short paragraphs, clear transitions, and only occasional unfamiliar words explained immediately.
- For ages 5–6, optional parent notes may connect events across the story or point out how the dream is fulfilled.
- Do not invent scenes, dialogue, thoughts, motives, sensory details, or timelines to make the story more dramatic.
- Keep references to wrongdoing and imprisonment non-graphic. The household incident in 12:23–35 should be handled briefly and respectfully, without sexual detail.

## Parent-facing source summary

Surah Yusuf itself provides the main sequence. It includes Yusuf's dream and Ya'qub's response (12:4–6); his brothers' plot and his removal from his family (12:7–18); his arrival in Egypt (12:19–22); the household incident, evidence, and imprisonment (12:23–35); the prison companions and dreams (12:36–42); the ruler's dream and its interpretation (12:43–49); Yusuf's vindication and responsibility (12:50–57); the brothers' first visit and later family developments (12:58–82); Ya'qub's grief and hope and the family's eventual return (12:83–98); and the reunion and Yusuf's reflection on his dream (12:99–101). This is a planned age adaptation, not a claim that every detail is equally suitable for every child.

## Values arc

- **Patience (sabr):** continue to seek what is right through difficulty, without implying passivity or that children should endure unsafe situations.
- **Trust in Allah (tawakkul):** ask Allah for help while taking appropriate action.
- **Integrity:** refuse wrongdoing and value truth.
- **Seeking and using knowledge:** Yusuf interprets dreams by the knowledge Allah grants him and advises practical preparation.
- **Responsibility:** the plan for the years of plenty and hardship provides for people.
- **Forgiveness and family repair:** Yusuf's later response to his brothers is a central theme; do not suggest forgiveness requires hiding harm or accepting unsafe behaviour.
- **Gratitude:** Yusuf acknowledges Allah's care in his final reflection.

## Chronology, pacing, and repetition

- Follow the Qur'anic order; do not rearrange events to create a suspense plot.
- Each section should have one main event and one moral.
- A recurring parent-facing connection may point back to the dream, but avoid repeating the same lesson in every section.
- The outline covers the broad account in 10 manageable sections. If the child finds sections 2, 4, or 8 too intense, pause or revise with the parent rather than softening the source into something misleading.

## Do not include

- Names of the eleven stars, or claims that identify each star, sun, and moon beyond what the Qur'an itself later explains. Tafsir Ibn Kathir's page on 12:4 discusses reports about star names and notes weakness in a related report; do not use such names.
- Exact ages, elapsed years, dates, locations beyond what the source establishes, or detailed descriptions of Yusuf's appearance.
- Invented dialogue, private thoughts, motives, feelings, or sensory scenes attributed to Yusuf, Ya'qub, the brothers, or other historical figures.
- Popular embellishments about the well, caravan, palace, prison, or reunion that are not supported by the recorded sources.
- Graphic violence, explicit sexual content, or an extended account of the household incident.
- A claim that patience means staying silent about danger, that forgiveness means allowing further harm, or that good behaviour guarantees an easy outcome.
- Any illustration depicting a prophet or Allah.

## Open questions for parent review

1. Is a 10-section treatment the right scope, or should the household incident and prison period be shortened further for this age range?
2. Should the book use “Prophet Yusuf (peace be upon him)” throughout, or another consistent respectful convention?
3. Is the moral for section 3 appropriately worded as a reflection rather than a direct sourced statement?
4. Should the later brothers' encounter (sections 8–9) be simplified further while preserving the Qur'anic sequence?
