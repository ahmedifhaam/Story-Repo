# Storytelling Guide (Ages 3–6)

## Audience and length

Write for a caregiver to read aloud to a child between ages 3 and 6. Each section should take about **4–5 minutes** aloud. As a rough starting point, aim for about 400–600 words, then read it aloud and adjust for pace, pauses, and the child's attention. Word count is a guide, not a target to pad.

Each section should be a satisfying small story moment, not a chapter that requires a long memory of earlier events.

## Two levels without two separate books

Keep the main story understandable to a 3-year-old. Support older children with optional extras:
- **For curious listeners (ages 5–6):** one short fact or connection supported by the sources.
- **Talk together:** one simple question with no single “test” answer.
- **Try it today:** one safe, age-appropriate way to practise the value.

Do not make these extras necessary to understand the story. Avoid babyish tone; respect the child as a growing learner.

## Language

- Prefer common, concrete English words and short sentences.
- Use active voice and clear subjects.
- Explain an occasional useful unfamiliar word at its first appearance: “generous (happy to share what you have).”
- Do not overload the story with brackets. Usually explain no more than 1–3 new words per section.
- Use Arabic terms when they are important to Islamic meaning, then explain them simply on first use.
- Avoid idioms that may confuse children learning English, sarcasm, abstract lectures, and long sentences with many clauses.
- Use respectful names and titles consistently, such as “Prophet Ibrahim (peace be upon him)” or “Prophet Muhammad ﷺ”. Choose a consistent convention within each book.
- Avoid frightening detail, graphic harm, intense threats, or lingering descriptions of suffering. Difficult events may be told gently and honestly without changing the source.

## Read-aloud craft

- Open with a clear image, question, sound, or small moment of curiosity.
- Use gentle repetition, patterns, and occasional short questions to invite attention.
- Give each section one main event or idea.
- Use sensory description only when it is harmless and not falsely presented as sourced historical detail. Clearly separate neutral scene-setting from claims about what actually happened.
- Use natural paragraph breaks and dialogue only when dialogue is sourced. Never invent dialogue for prophets or Companions.
- End with a calm, memorable closing line or transition to the next section.
- Avoid cliff-hangers that create fear or pressure the child to continue.

## One moral per section

End with:
1. **The lesson:** one short sentence in child-friendly language.
2. **Talk together:** an optional open question.
3. **Try it today:** optional practical action, especially for ages 5–6.

The moral must be genuinely connected to the sourced events. Do not force every story to teach the same lesson or suggest that good behaviour guarantees that life will always go the child's way.

## Emotional and religious care

- Present Allah with reverence and do not depict Allah.
- Encourage love, hope, gratitude, trust, kindness, honesty, patience, courage, fairness, and care.
- Avoid teaching that a child is bad or unloved when they make a mistake. Emphasise learning, repentance when relevant, and trying again.
- Avoid making frightening claims about punishment central to stories for this age range.
- Never frame another religion or group of people as a target for hatred or mockery.
- Be truthful about hard events while keeping descriptions non-graphic and emotionally safe.
- Do not pretend that a prophet or historical figure behaved or spoke in a way the sources do not establish.

## Illustrations

Never depict any prophet, even symbolically as a human silhouette. Do not depict Allah. Prefer:
- landscapes, skies, mountains, rivers, deserts, gardens, and stars;
- buildings, roads, boats, wells, tents, tools, clothing, and objects;
- maps, symbolic patterns, plants, and animals when appropriate to the source.

For non-prophet historical figures, use respectful, non-sensational imagery and avoid presenting invented visual details as historically certain. Add an illustration note to each section; if uncertain, choose a setting-only scene.

## Suggested section format

# Section N — [Short title]

[4–5 minute read-aloud story]

**A word to know:** [optional word and simple definition]

**The lesson:** [one clear moral]

**Talk together:** [optional question]

**Try it today:** [optional practical activity]

**Illustration note:** [setting-focused description; no prophets]

**Sources:** [short source references linked to the full book source register]
