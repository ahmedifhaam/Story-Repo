# Islamic Source and Authenticity Policy

## Purpose

This project follows a mainstream Sunni approach and prioritises trustworthy religious sources over dramatic storytelling. These rules apply to research, outlines, stories, morals, captions, illustrations, and metadata.

## Source order

1. **The Qur'an.** Read the relevant passage in context. Record the surah and ayah range. Use a recognised translation for English meaning and, where a detail is important, compare reliable translations and tafsir.
2. **Authentic hadith.** Prefer narrations in Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim when relevant. Other Sunni hadith collections may be used only after checking the specific narration, its grading, attribution, and context through reliable scholarly references.
3. **Recognised tafsir and scholarly explanation.** Use established Sunni tafsir to clarify Qur'anic context, not to smuggle uncertain reports into the story. Record the work, author, relevant passage, and a stable URL or edition details.
4. **Reliable seerah and history.** For the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ and the Companions, use established works and reputable scholarly research. Verify specific reports rather than treating every report in a historical book as authentic.
5. **Secondary educational sources.** Use reputable institutions to discover references or clarify context. They do not replace checking the underlying source when a claim is central.

## Evidence labels

Assign each material claim one label in the research notes:

- **QUR'AN:** stated directly in a cited Qur'anic passage.
- **SAHIH HADITH:** supported by a specific hadith verified as authentic.
- **OTHER VERIFIED REPORT:** supported by a report whose status and context have been checked; document the evidence and scholarly assessment.
- **INTERPRETIVE CONTEXT:** an explanation drawn from named tafsir or scholarship, not a direct quotation from scripture.
- **UNVERIFIED / DISPUTED:** uncertain, weak, conflicting, or not adequately checked. Do not present it as fact in a child's story.

Do not use labels as a substitute for citations. Keep a claim-to-source table in each book's `sources.md`.

## Non-negotiable rules

- Never fabricate a hadith, Qur'an quotation, reference, chain of narration, scholar's view, URL, or authenticity grading.
- Never imply that a popular story is authentic merely because it appears on many websites.
- Do not invent dialogue, private thoughts, motives, emotional reactions, scenery, dates, names, or events and attribute them to a prophet or Companion.
- Do not merge versions of a story without noting important differences.
- Do not use weak or disputed reports as factual plot points simply because they make the story more exciting.
- If scholars differ, record the disagreement and either omit the detail from the child's story or phrase it carefully with parent-facing context.
- Distinguish a direct quotation from a paraphrase. For Qur'anic meanings, identify the translation used. Do not write a paraphrase inside quotation marks.
- A moral must be consistent with the source and must not be presented as a hadith unless it actually is one.
- Do not invent a “lesson Allah taught” beyond what the sources support.
- When evidence is insufficient, state **“Not verified — leave out unless reviewed”** and continue without that detail.

## Research process

1. Search for the primary passage or narration first.
2. Open and read the relevant source in context; do not rely only on search snippets.
3. Record exact references and working URLs, access date, translation/edition, and relevant source notes.
4. For hadith, verify collection, hadith number as used by that edition/database, narrator when relevant, and grading from a credible reference. Hadith numbering can vary by edition, so include a stable URL or chapter as well.
5. Cross-check central details against at least one reputable scholarly source when practical.
6. Build a claim-to-source table before writing the outline.
7. Mark any detail not supported by a source. Exclude it from the narrative.
8. Recheck the final section against the evidence table.

## Research source suggestions

Use primary and reputable references where accessible, for example:

- Qur'an text and translations: Quran.com (check the translation shown and cite the surah and ayah).
- Hadith research: Sunnah.com for searchable collections and references; confirm grading and context, especially outside Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim.
- Tafsir: recognised editions of Tafsir Ibn Kathir and other established Sunni tafsir, consulting reliable translations and scholarly context.
- Seerah and Companion history: established scholarly works and reputable educational institutions, with individual reports checked rather than accepted wholesale.

These are starting points, not automatic guarantees that every page or report is correct. Research must evaluate the specific claim.

## Parent-facing research record

For every book, `sources.md` should include:
- Source ID
- Claim or story event supported
- Evidence label
- Primary reference (surah/ayah or collection/chapter/hadith number)
- Translation, edition, or scholar
- URL and date accessed
- Authenticity/context notes
- Whether the claim is used in the child-facing story

## If a source cannot be reached

Do not pretend to have searched it. Record the access limitation and use a source that can actually be inspected. If the AI session has no web access, ask for research access or clearly mark the outline as **research incomplete**; do not call it research-backed until the sources have been checked.
